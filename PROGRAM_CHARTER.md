# Continuity Framework Program Charter

## Purpose

The Continuity Framework is a research, software, validation, benchmark, and product program for studying observable narrative patterns related to identity continuity and revision.

The framework does not diagnose people or infer hidden mental states. It analyzes language evidence in passages, conversations, and longitudinal text sequences.

## Core thesis

**Identity is continuity under revision.**

## Program components

1. Mid-Process Identity Loop theory
2. Human coding rubric
3. Continuity Engine reference implementation
4. Revision Latency longitudinal model
5. Validation studies
6. Research datasets and benchmark tasks
7. AI narrative-response evaluation toolkit
8. Publications and public documentation

## Strategic objective

Develop a transparent and independently testable framework that can identify and explain narrative patterns involving:

- identity fixity
- prediction and expectation
- threat amplification
- feedback integration
- narrative flexibility
- unresolved self-model conflict
- agency collapse
- continuity-preserving revision

## Initial product direction

The preferred first product is an **AI Narrative Response Evaluator** that assesses whether an AI response reinforces rigidity, threat, fragmentation, or agency loss, or supports evidence integration, boundaries, flexibility, and continuity-preserving revision.

The product evaluates text and conversations. It does not evaluate the worth, competence, personality, or mental health of users.

## Program principles

1. Freeze baselines before changing rules.
2. Separate calibration, regression, and held-out evaluation data.
3. Never tune against a held-out dataset after labels are revealed.
4. Treat abstention and human review as valid outputs.
5. Preserve a complete decision trace for every classification.
6. Report both improvements and regressions.
7. Validate human construct agreement before claiming automated validity.
8. Publish known failure modes rather than concealing them.
9. Prohibit coercive, diagnostic, punitive, or surveillance uses.
